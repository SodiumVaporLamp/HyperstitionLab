time-sorcery

The Ccru’s glossary defines time-sorcery as “counterchronic effectuation of the Numogram.” This page takes a phrase about the future and a year, and runs them through the number practices in the Ccru’s texts and Nick Land’s “Qabbala 101.” Each step says which part comes from those texts and which part is this page’s own.

Your reading

1

listen for signal

From the textsLand, “Qabbala 101,” pp. 272, 275, 284–85.

This pageNothing yet; this is the premise.

Land opens “Qabbala 101” by calling qabbalism “a practical programme, rather than a doctrine.” Its working hypothesis is that “signal from ‘outside the system’ is detectable by numerical analysis of codes circulating within the system,” and he compares its position to that of “a spontaneous artificial intelligence.” Near the end he notices that the very words used to argue about it keep landing on multiples of nine.

2

count

From the textsLand, pp. 277–79.

This pageCounting your phrase the same way.

The crudest procedure is to count letters, which Land calls Primitive Numerization. He runs it on the English names of the digits and finds that every syzygy, each pair of digits that sums to nine, has names totalling eight letters. If each name were randomly three, four or five letters long, a pair would reach eight in three cases out of nine, so all five pairs would do it about one time in 243. That is his figure, and the arithmetic holds.

3

total

From the textsLand, p. 281 and n. 4.

This pageTotalling your phrase.

Land’s footnote credits “August Barrow’s ‘Anglossic Qabbala’,” whose basic tool counts digits and letters as one sequence: 0 to 9 keep their values, then A = 10 through Z = 35. His example is UNITY = 30 + 23 + 18 + 29 + 34 = 134.

4

plex

From the textsLand, pp. 279–81; Ccru glossary, “Syzygy.”

This pagePlexing your total.

Next he adds the digits: 1 + 3 + 4 = 8. He calls 8 the “Numogrammatic twin” of numerology’s 1 for unity, because 8 and 1 sum to nine, which makes them a syzygy on the Numogram. Keep adding until one digit is left: that digit is the zone. A total that plexes to 9 stands for the 9::0 pair, since a digital root never gives 0.

5

date

From the textsCcru glossary, “K-Time”; Ccru, Zone-9 page.

This pagePlexing years into zones, and the spiral.

The Ccru glossary defines K-Time as the “native calendar of cyberspace”: a year count, “initially binomic,” that starts from 00 = AD 1900. Two-digit year fields in software counted exactly this way until the Y2K scare, and the Ccru’s Zone-9 page reads ninety-nine, “as dramatized by the Y2K panic,” as “the cyclic completion of time.” Count your year from 1900 and plex it like a phrase. On the spiral one turn is nine years, so years in the same zone share a spoke.

6

name the distance

From the textsCcru glossary, “Time-Sorcery”; Ccru, Pandemonium Matrix.

This pagePairing a phrase with a year, and the turn-by-turn table.

The glossary’s definition goes on: time-sorcery works “through spiral involvement with demonic distances as transmutations of time.” Every pair of zones has a demon in the Pandemonium Matrix, so the distance between your phrase and your year has a name.

7

walk the rites

From the textsCcru, Pandemonium Matrix (rite paths); Ccru glossary, “Rite.”

This pageThe three-move reading, the backwards direction and the shortest passage.

Each demon has rites, which the glossary describes as “a line of passage across the Numogram,” written as strings of zones. Lurgo’s rite [1890] runs from 1 across its syzygy to 8, through the eighth gate to 9, and across to 0. All 75 published rite paths can be walked with three moves: crossing a syzygy, riding its current, or taking a gate. “Counterchronic” suggests the future acting on the present, so this page reads the passage backwards, from your year’s zone to your phrase’s.

8

xenotate

From the textsLand, “Tic-Talk,” in Fanged Noumena.

This pageXenotating your totals and counting their colons.

Decimal digits are a convention too. In Land’s “Tic-Talk,” Daniel Barker devises Tic Xenotation for a SETI-style project, as a way of writing numbers free of any culture’s habits. A colon is 2, writing things side by side multiplies them, and brackets around a number x mean the x-th prime. So 3 = (:), 5 = ((:)) and 6 = :(:). Drawn out, every number becomes a cluster of nested cells.

9

transmit

From the textsCcru glossary, “Hyperstition”; Land, p. 275; Ccru, Pandemonium Matrix.

This pageThe transmission format.

The glossary defines hyperstition as an “element of effective culture that makes itself real, through fictional quantities functioning as time-travelling potentials.” Land says qabbalism itself was “compelled to hyperstitionally generate an extreme antiquity for itself,” and the Pandemonium Matrix describes the Necronomicon as “retrodeposited out of the future into the deep past.” A reading only does something once it circulates. Add one line and send it forward.
