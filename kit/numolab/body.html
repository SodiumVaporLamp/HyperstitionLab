What this is

Gematria gives each letter a number, so every word adds up to a value, and words with equal values are read as linked. It comes from Hebrew tradition. AQ is the English cipher of the Ccru, the group around Nick Land, Sadie Plant and Mark Fisher at Warwick in the late 1990s, whose work mixed philosophy, cybernetics, occultism and science fiction.

The Numogram is their map of the ten digits. Ccru texts present it as recovered rather than invented, growing out of a Professor Barker’s study of nine-sum twinning, which promised “a secret door into the long-hidden crypt of the decimal system.” Barker is probably one of their own fictions. They worked the diagram as a practical instrument: a map of time with a looping Time-Circuit and an Outside, and a register of 45 demons. Part of the appeal is the appeal of divination. Fixed rules hand you readings you didn’t choose, and the meaning comes from what you make of them. The Ccru’s word for fictions that make themselves real through use was hyperstition, and the Numogram was one of its main instruments.

Inherited and added. The diagram, its zones, currents, gates and demons, and the AQ cipher come from Ccru texts. Reading AQ totals through the diagram has a published precedent in Nick Land’s “Qabbala 101” (Collapse I, 2006). A footnote totals UNITY as 30 + 23 + 18 + 29 + 34 = 134 and reduces it to 8, which the text calls the Numogrammatic twin of 1, numerology’s number for unity. Whether the wider Ccru worked this way is a separate question. The phrase pairs, searches and experiments built on top are this page’s own.

Nine and zero. A digital root runs from 1 to 9 and never gives 0, because 9 and 0 are the same number in arithmetic mod 9. The diagram draws them as two zones joined by Uttunul’s syzygy. Whenever the page puts a phrase in zone 9, read it as that 9::0 pair.

Try a pair

For a phrase of letters alone, Ordinal, Reduction and AQ always agree on the zone. Reverse and Reverse reduction land in its syzygy twin; for a phrase on 9, that twin is 0, which a digital root also writes as 9. Satanic shifts the zone back one step for every letter. Digits break the pattern: AQ counts them at face value and the other ciphers skip them. Values the two phrases share are shaded in the card.

The line under the demon’s name is this page’s paraphrase of its entry in the Ccru’s Pandemonium Matrix; the Demons tab adds its rites. For a fuller reading of the demon between two names, with each Lemur’s titles and descriptions, try the Divination mode in Cyphers’ Numogram, which inspired this page.

alphabet

In AQ every mirror pair of letters, A/Z through M/N, sums to 45, the number of the final gate. So a letter and its mirror always land in the same syzygy: on twin zones, or both on 9 for I and R.

Letters by zone

No letter plexes to 0, because a digital root never does. I and R, both on 9, stand for the whole 9::0 syzygy.

Mirror a word

Reverse Ordinal scores every letter as its mirror, so a word’s Reverse Ordinal always plexes into the twin of its Ordinal zone.

Gate names point home

G and T are mirror letters, so “Gt” totals 45, which plexes to nothing. Written out in AQ, every gate’s name plexes to the zone it opens onto.

demons come home

Each of the Pandemonium Matrix’s 45 demons stands between two zones. Total a demon’s name in AQ, plex it, and see whether it lands in one of its own two zones. Then ask how surprising the pattern is.

loops

Cipher-hop loops: one phrase’s value in one cipher equals the next phrase’s value in another, all the way back to the start. Each phrase leaves by a different cipher than it entered, and exact twins can’t share a loop.

        Rules

        AQ
        A = 10 through Z = 35, with digits 0–9 at face value. Accents are dropped before counting.
        Other ciphers
        Ordinal A = 1 to Z = 26. Reverse Z = 1 to A = 26. Reduction and Reverse reduction collapse each letter to one digit. Satanic A = 36 to Z = 61. These five count letters only and skip digits.
        Zones
        A phrase’s zone is the digital root of its AQ total. Digital roots run from 1 to 9: 9 and 0 are the same number mod 9, so anything that would land on 0 shows as 9 and stands for the 9::0 syzygy. Only an empty phrase gets 0.
        Numogram
        Syzygies sum to nine. Each syzygy’s current flows to the zone named by its difference: Sink, Surge, Hold, Warp and Plex. A gate leads from zone n to the digital root of 0 + 1 + … + n.
        Chance
        Two random phrases share a zone about one time in nine, and land on syzygy twins about one time in ten. The lab finds coincidences; weighing them is up to you.
